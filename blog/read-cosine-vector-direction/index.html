Read Cosine Similarity Through Direction, Coordinate Meaning and Zero Vectors

Read Cosine Similarity Through Direction, Coordinate Meaning and Zero Vectors

A cosine score can help compare the direction of two numerical vectors. Reading that score as a verdict about an answer requires much more evidence. The coordinates might describe word counts, deliberately constructed features, or representations produced by a particular embedding model. Their meaning comes from the construction process. A number calculated from those coordinates cannot establish that the documents say the same thing, that a retrieved passage answers the question, or that a proposed action is authorized. Start an AI-assisted comparison by naming the representation and the narrower question the calculation can answer.

This guide uses original fictional numerical examples. None of the vectors below came from a language model, embedding service, search index, client document or production evaluation. The calculations illustrate consequences of specified inputs. They do not measure semantic similarity, retrieval usefulness or business performance. An assistant can help explain the arithmetic and organize questions about a real representation, but the evidence for an answer must still come from inspected material and an appropriate evaluation.

Name the score before comparing reports

SciPy's official cosine interface documents cosine distance as one minus the dot product divided by the product of the two Euclidean norms. That interface description supplies a useful vocabulary boundary: the returned distance and its similarity complement have different directions. Under the unweighted formula used here, similarity is the normalized dot product and distance is one minus similarity. A report that merely says cosine and prints a number leaves an avoidable ambiguity. Ask whether a larger number means closer direction or greater distance before sorting candidates.

Write the actual formula in the comparison note. For vectors u and v, calculate the dot product by multiplying corresponding coordinates and adding those products. Calculate each norm by taking the square root of the sum of its squared coordinates. Divide the dot product by the product of those norms when both are nonzero. The coordinate pairing matters at every stage. Merely having the same number of entries does not establish that the entries describe the same features in the same order.

Consider a report that labels its value 0.8 but does not identify the convention. Similarity 0.8 has distance complement 0.2 under the declared formula. Distance 0.8 has similarity complement 0.2. These are different directional relationships. Changing the displayed label without checking the producer's definition can reverse an interpretation. Preserve the original field name, formula and version in an AI prompt, and ask the assistant to explain the convention rather than silently choose one.

Example A: identical direction can hide a magnitude difference

Use the fictional vectors u = [1, 2] and v = [2, 4]. Their dot product is 10. Their squared norms are 5 and 20, so the product of their norms is the square root of 100, which is 10. Their cosine similarity is therefore 1 and their cosine distance is 0. Their coordinate differences are still [1, 2]. The second vector has twice the norm of the first. Equal direction has not made the original quantities equal.

If the coordinates represented invented counts in two categories, a reader concerned with the number of items would need those counts as well as the directional comparison. If the vectors were model representations, deciding whether their magnitudes carry useful information would require documentation and task evidence. The teaching calculation does not settle that choice. It demonstrates why a phrase such as exact match is too broad for a directional score of 1.

A useful explanation would state both facts together: the supplied second vector is a positive multiple of the first, giving cosine similarity 1 under the declared formula, while the original coordinates differ. Ask AI to retain the original vectors next to its explanation. This makes it possible to inspect what normalization removed from the comparison. It also prevents an abbreviated summary from replacing the original numerical record with a stronger assertion about document identity.

Example B: opposite direction is a numerical relationship

Keep u = [1, 2] and change v to [-2, -4]. The dot product is -10 and the product of the norms remains 10. Similarity is -1 and distance is 2. The signed coordinates allow this opposite direction. A claim that every cosine distance must lie between zero and one would fail for these supplied inputs. A claim about a particular representation with additional constraints needs those constraints stated explicitly.

The result does not mean the second document is false, hostile, low quality or an instruction to reverse the first. Those are interpretations of content or purpose. Here, the only evidence is an authored pair of numerical arrays. Even when real embedding vectors contain negative coordinates, the interpretation of their positions depends on how they were produced and evaluated. A mathematical extreme is not a substitute for reading the relevant passage.

Keep a distinction between the range permitted by a formula and the range observed in a particular collection. A collection might happen to contain only positive similarities without changing the formula's broader possibilities. Conversely, a negative value in one task need not imply a universal semantic category. Ask an assistant to describe the supplied coordinate relationship first and to identify any additional content claim as a separate question requiring evidence.

Example C: preserve the coordinate labels

Give u the labeled coordinates Cedar = 1 and Maple = 0. Give v Cedar = 1 and Maple = 0 as well. With that shared order, their similarity is 1. If an incoming row for v is arranged Maple first and Cedar second, its displayed values are [0, 1]. Comparing that row positionally against u = [1, 0] yields dot product 0 and similarity 0. Aligning the supplied feature labels restores v = [1, 0] and similarity 1.

The arithmetic shows the consequence of two declared alignments. It does not prove that Cedar and Maple in an actual system are correctly identified features. Labels can themselves be stale, ambiguous or transformed. Before asking AI to compare exported vectors, establish what each position means and whether that mapping is shared. Preserve the schema or feature mapping alongside the arrays rather than relying on the assistant to infer it from plausible names.

This is also why copying a vector from another embedding model into an existing comparison is not justified by matching its length. An array with the same number of coordinates may use a different representation. This guide does not assert that a particular pair of models is compatible or incompatible; it identifies missing evidence. A defensible report names the producing representation and checks whether the comparison is defined for that pair before presenting a score as meaningful.

Example D: a zero vector needs an explicit decision

Let u = [0, 0] and v = [3, 4]. The dot product is 0, but the product of the norms is also 0. The raw normalized-dot-product formula therefore asks for 0 divided by 0, which is undefined. Replacing this with similarity 0 would introduce a convention. It would not follow from the arithmetic. A different application might reject the input or apply its own documented handling. Identify that behavior from the actual implementation instead of predicting it from this example.

The business meaning of the zero vector is a separate issue. It could result from a deliberate construction rule, an empty selected feature set, a transformation or an error. The supplied numbers alone do not reveal the cause. An assistant should preserve the unresolved state and request the representation's documented behavior. Calling the record unrelated, absent or successfully processed would collapse several different questions into one unsupported status.

Record both the numerical condition and the next evidence needed. For example, a draft note could say that the supplied vector has zero norm, the raw cosine formula is undefined, and the producer's handling and the record's construction history have not been checked. This wording keeps the calculation useful without claiming that a missing or unusual record has been diagnosed. It also makes a later correction easier to trace.

Example E: a common offset can change the angle

Begin with u = [1, 0] and v = [0, 1]. Their similarity is 0. Add 1 to every coordinate of both vectors, obtaining [2, 1] and [1, 2]. Their dot product is 4 and each squared norm is 5, so similarity is 4/5. The two coordinate differences remain [-1, 1]. A common additive shift has changed the cosine relationship even though the corresponding differences have stayed the same.

This example separates cosine from a comparison based only on coordinate differences. It also separates cosine from centered correlation. The guide's Pearson and Spearman companions address other questions and should not be treated as alternative names for this calculation. Before an AI summary compares scores produced before and after a transformation, identify the transformation and recompute the intended quantity on the declared inputs.

A preprocessing change can therefore matter without demonstrating that the represented documents changed. Conversely, unchanged documents do not guarantee unchanged vectors if their producing pipeline changes. Keep representation version, preprocessing and scoring convention together. If a report loses that context, return to the producer's evidence rather than guessing a universal conversion between the old and new scores.

Example F: missing coordinates leave the full comparison unresolved

Use u = [1, 0, 1] and a partially supplied v = [1, 0, unknown]. On the two known coordinates, the compared vectors [1, 0] and [1, 0] have similarity 1. That is a calculation on a selected two-coordinate subset. Two of the three coordinate pairs are available, giving availability 2/3. Neither fact establishes the full three-coordinate cosine similarity.

For an explicitly hypothetical completion, set the unknown coordinate to 1. The full vectors then match and similarity is 1. For another hypothetical completion, set it to -1. The dot product becomes 0, with both squared norms equal to 2, giving similarity 0. These alternatives demonstrate dependence on the missing coordinate. They are not predictions, imputations or observed embedding outputs.

Do not combine a full vector on one side with a silently shortened vector on the other. Do not describe a subset score as the full score unless a documented procedure defines that selection and the report clearly names it. An AI assistant can list which coordinates were available and explain the hypothetical alternatives, while leaving the full result unresolved. The next step is obtaining the actual missing value or following an explicitly justified handling rule.

Prepare a bounded AI explanation request

Give the assistant the arrays, coordinate labels, formula and the question you are trying to answer. For a numerical teaching exercise, an original instruction could be: Explain the unweighted cosine calculation for the supplied fictional vectors, show the dot product and squared norms, and distinguish direction from equality of the original coordinates. If either norm is zero or an input is missing, preserve that condition instead of inventing a replacement value. This is an authored prompt suggestion, not an observed model response or a tested guarantee of assistant behavior.

For a real retrieval review, include the query, the inspected candidate passage and the relevance question separately from any numerical score. An assistant can prepare a comparison note that leaves room for the reader's judgment. It should not be asked to infer the passage's content from a vector alone. If the question is whether a source supports a claim, inspect the source and the claim directly. A directional relationship in a representation does not supply a missing quotation, a factual reference or an authorization record.

Keep numerical checking and content checking as two explicit activities. In example A, arithmetic can confirm that the dot product and denominator both equal 10. Determining whether an actual passage answers an actual question would require a task-specific judgment on that material. Passing the first activity does not complete the second. When the assistant drafts a concise explanation, ask it to identify which statements came from the supplied numerical inputs and which questions still require source inspection.

The same separation helps when a score is copied into a management presentation. An explanation such as similarity is 1 because the supplied vectors are positive multiples is supported by the teaching inputs. A sentence saying the answers are interchangeable adds a content judgment. A sentence saying the workflow can run without review adds an operating decision. Preserve the first statement and route the other questions to their actual evidence and decision owners rather than allowing a compact slide to merge them.

Compare candidate rankings within a defined evaluation

A retrieval system may use a similarity score to order candidates, but an ordered candidate list and a useful final answer are different objects. The selected cutoff, candidate collection, relevance judgments and downstream use all influence the evaluation question. The companion guides on retrieval precision and recall, reciprocal rank and average precision explain ways to read labeled rankings. Their metrics still require a defensible relevance reference. A cosine calculation does not create that reference.

Suppose a reviewer wants to compare two production configurations. Preserve the query set, source collection, producing representation, normalization, scoring convention and cutoff for each configuration. Identify which of those components changed before assigning a reason to a ranking difference. This guide does not provide a causal experiment or a universal evaluation design. It gives the reader a way to avoid describing a change in the representation as if it were already a measured change in answer quality.

A threshold also needs an explicit scope. A number selected for one represented collection is not established as a suitable cutoff for another merely because both systems use the word cosine. If a real team has documented judgments and an agreed decision rule, evaluate the rule on the intended material. If that evidence has not been provided, describe the cutoff as proposed or inherited rather than validated. Do not turn a teaching value in this article into a production recommendation.

Preserve nearby candidates when reviewing a difficult selection. Their inspected content can reveal why a numerical ordering is or is not useful for the actual task. Recording only the winning score removes the comparison that the reviewer needs. An assistant may help arrange the candidate text, source locations and open questions, while the evaluation remains anchored to the task and the actual passages. This avoids substituting a mathematical label for an unexplained content choice.

Make a correction traceable

If a coordinate-order mistake is found, save the original arrays and their stated mapping before preparing a corrected comparison. Explain the changed mapping and recompute the same declared formula. Example C supplies a narrow illustration: the positional calculation gives 0, while the explicitly aligned feature calculation gives 1. This does not establish that a real import has been repaired. A real correction needs the producer's mapping and a check that the corrected record reached the intended destination.

If the producer changes its representation, keep the old and new versions named in the report. A newly calculated value should not silently overwrite the earlier value while retaining an explanation based on the old pipeline. Distinguish an input correction, a transformation change, a scoring-convention change and a new source record. Those events may all alter a displayed number, but they answer different questions about what happened.

An explanation can remain useful when it ends with an unresolved issue. For example, a reader may know the formula and the coordinate labels but still lack the third coordinate in example F. Showing the two hypothetical completions explains why the missing value matters. Leaving the full score open is then an informative result of the supplied evidence, rather than a failure to produce a tidy number. Preserve that reasoning when shortening the note for another reader.

Frequently asked questions

Does cosine similarity 1 mean two documents are identical?

It establishes the corresponding directional relationship under the declared numerical formula for valid nonzero inputs. Example A has similarity 1 even though every displayed coordinate in the second vector is twice the corresponding coordinate in the first. Document identity requires evidence about the actual documents. Semantic equivalence requires a suitable content judgment. Neither follows from the authored numerical example, and a score alone should not replace the original material in a review.

Can cosine similarity be negative?

Yes, the signed numerical example B has normalized dot product -1, with distance complement 2. Whether a particular representation and collection produce negative values is a question about those inputs and their construction. The sign does not by itself certify that content is false, opposed in meaning or unsuitable. Read the producer's convention and inspect the task evidence before attaching a content label to the score.

Should a zero vector receive a similarity of zero?

The raw formula is undefined when the denominator is zero. Assigning a numeric result introduces a handling convention that must be identified from the actual application or explicitly declared for the task. The zero vector's origin also needs evidence. Do not infer an empty document, a failed model call or an unrelated passage from the vector alone. Preserve the numerical condition and the unanswered construction question separately.

Why does coordinate order matter if the arrays have the same length?

The dot product multiplies corresponding coordinates. Those pairings need a shared meaning. Example C yields different calculations when a row is compared positionally and when its supplied feature labels are aligned. Equal length establishes neither the alignment nor compatibility between separately produced representations. Retain the feature mapping or representation contract alongside the arrays, and resolve any mismatch before interpreting the score.

Is a score on available coordinates the full score?

It is a score on the selected coordinates unless a documented procedure explicitly defines something else. Example F has subset similarity 1, while two hypothetical completions give different full-vector values. The availability fraction 2/3 counts known coordinate pairs and does not measure semantic coverage. Obtain the missing input or follow an explicit justified handling rule before presenting a full comparison as resolved.

Can an AI assistant use this guide to approve a retrieval system?

The guide can support a clearer explanation request and a narrower arithmetic review. It provides no model evaluation, source-relevance certification, business outcome or operating approval. A release decision needs evidence for the intended task, inspected sources, relevant evaluation judgments and the appropriate decision process. Use the guide with the evaluation-dataset and retrieval-metric companions to frame those questions, while preserving the distinction between supplied-input calculations and an actual system assessment.
